Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 2 first, looking out of a window. My close up try was too intense on the first go, all shadow and no face. The window one is gentler. Soft light on one side, the rest falling away, and your face still clearly yours.

Colour can hide a lot. Take it away, and all that is left is the face, the light, and the mood. That is why black and white portraits feel so honest.

These prompts start from a clear photo of you. The tool turns it into a film style portrait, with strong side light, deep shadows, or a plain background, and keeps your face looking like you.

My first black and white try was a flop. I used a blurry evening photo, and without colour there was nowhere for the blur to hide. A sharp daylight photo fixed it in one go.

Some are quiet, like looking out of a window. Some are full of feeling, like laughing in natural light. Pick the mood that fits the photo you want, not the mood you think sounds artistic.

Before you start

Black and white is simple, but it is not forgiving. Five points before you start, and the first one matters most.

  • Use a clear photo with good contrast, taken in daylight, with your face in focus and looking at the camera.
  • A plain background in the starting photo helps. Black and white shows clutter more than colour does.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Pick a photo with a clear expression. A blank face looks flatter without colour.
  • Only use a photo you have the right to use.

Pick the mood you like, copy the prompt, and paste it with your photo. The window one is my pick.

1. Close up, strong side light

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you in a close up black and white portrait, with strong side light, deep shadows, and a plain dark background. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

Why this works

Strong side light shapes the face, and black and white makes that shape the whole picture. The close crop keeps nothing in the way.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under the photo.
  4. Send it and wait.
  5. Check the face and the shadows first. If the face looks right, save the portrait.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. If the shadows are too dark or too light, ask for that one change only.

Black and white forgives a lot, but it punishes a blurry starting photo. If the result looks soft, try again with a sharper photo. Mine went from mush to a keeper just by swapping the starting shot.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with a sharp photo. Black and white shows blur more than colour does.
  • Look for light and shadow in your starting photo. A photo with some contrast turns out best.
  • Keep the prompt simple on the first try. Extra detail can muddy the tones.
  • Check the eyes first in the result. In black and white, the eyes carry the portrait.
  • Ask for a vertical photo if you want it as a profile picture or a print.
  • Save the prompt that worked. The same style will suit another photo later.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not start with a dark or blurry photo. The tool will guess at your face, and black and white will not hide it.
  • Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
  • Do not change the whole prompt at once. Change the light or the background, not both.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. Run it again if the first try is not right.
  • Do not add colour words to the prompt. The whole point is black and white.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the portrait for a post, a profile photo, or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini is often good with a reference photo, and ChatGPT is often good with a clear, detailed prompt. Try the same prompt in both and keep the portrait you like best.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is usually the starting photo. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess the face. Use a clear front photo in good light and keep the prompt as it is on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the light or the pose to fit your photo. Keep the part about keeping the face the same, and keep the scene simple.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a post on your own social media, a profile photo, or a print. Only use a photo you have the right to use.
