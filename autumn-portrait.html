Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 3 first, walking on a leaf covered path. I ran it with a photo of myself in a brown coat, taken on an ordinary walk, and the crunch of leaves underfoot somehow came through in the picture. It looked like a walk I had actually taken, not a scene I had asked for.

My favourite autumn photo was not planned at all. I was out on a walk, leaves crunching under my shoes, cold wind on my face, and a friend took a photo of me laughing at something. I have liked it more than any posed picture since. That feeling is what these prompts are built around.

The first time I tested this set, I used a photo taken in harsh midday sun and the leaves came out looking orange in a way that felt fake. I swapped in a softer photo, late afternoon, same coat, and the whole thing settled down. Autumn light is low and warm, and your starting photo should be too.

You do not need a forest or a fancy camera. A clear photo of you in daylight is enough. The prompt adds the leaves, the coat, the warm light, and the rest. Pick the scene that sounds most like a walk you would actually take.

Try one, look at the result properly, and if something feels off, change one small thing and try again. The best autumn photo is usually the simplest one, a path, a bench, a cup of tea.

Before you start

Autumn photos live or die on the light, so get these five things right first.

  • Use a clear photo of you in daylight, face in focus, looking at the camera. Harsh midday sun makes the leaves look fake.
  • Wear something warm in your starting photo if you can. A coat or a scarf helps the scene sit naturally.
  • Keep the background plain in your starting photo. A busy street behind you fights with the leaves.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use a photo of yourself, or of someone who is happy for you to use it.

10 copy and paste prompts

Pick the autumn scene that sounds like you, a walk, a bench, a cup of tea, and paste the prompt under your photo.

1. Among autumn leaves in a park

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you standing among autumn leaves in a park, in a warm coat, with red and gold leaves around you. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it and wait. Do not send it again while it is still working.
  5. Check the face first, then the leaves and the light. If the face looks right, keep the photo.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. If it is close but the light feels wrong, ask for one change only, such as warmer light, and keep the rest the same.

If the leaves in your result look too orange or too perfect, swap in a softer starting photo, one taken in late afternoon light. That one change fixed the fake look in my tests more than any prompt tweak did.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use a photo taken in soft light, late afternoon if you can. Harsh sun made my first test look fake.
  • Wear a warm coat or a scarf in your starting photo. It helps the autumn scene sit naturally.
  • Start with the path or the bench prompts. They are the most reliable, because the scene is simple and the light is steady.
  • Check the leaves near your face and hair in the result. That is where the tool sometimes drops a leaf where it should not be.
  • Keep the colours warm but not loud. If the result looks too orange, ask for softer light and try again.
  • Save the prompt that worked. The same autumn style will suit a different coat next year.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not start with a photo taken in harsh midday sun. The leaves come out looking fake and the shadows are hard.
  • Do not add too many autumn things at once. Leaves, pumpkins, a scarf and rain in one photo is too much. Pick one.
  • Do not rewrite the whole prompt at once. Change one thing, the setting or the light, and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. I ran the path prompt three times and got three different walks. Keep the best.
  • Do not use a busy street as your starting background. The tool has to remove it, and the leaves end up in odd places.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free, plus the 3 bonus ones further down the page. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your photo, and use the picture for a post or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I use both. Gemini kept my face closer on the path prompt, while ChatGPT followed the forest light better. Run the same prompt in both and keep the picture you like best.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is almost always the starting photo. If it is dark, blurry, or taken from the side, the tool has to guess the face. Use a clear front photo in good light and leave the prompt alone on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the coat, or the light to fit your photo. Keep the line about keeping the face the same, and keep the scene simple so the tool has less to get wrong.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a post on your own social media or a print. Only use a photo of someone else if they are happy for you to use it.
