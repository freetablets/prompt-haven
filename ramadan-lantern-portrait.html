Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 1 first, holding a lantern at dusk. The light is gentle on the face, the scene is simple, and it comes out right more often than the busier scenes.

Ramadan evenings have a light of their own. The sky turns soft just before iftar, the lanterns come out, and everything slows down for a while. I wanted prompts that feel like that, calm and warm, not loud or busy.

These prompts start from one photo of you. The tool places you beside a lantern, on a rooftop at dusk, or in a quiet corner with prayer beads, and keeps your face exactly the same. That is the whole point, it should still be you.

I tested these with my own photo first, and the rooftop one took two tries. My first photo was taken in a dark room, and the lantern light kept washing my face out. A brighter starting photo fixed it straight away, so start with a good one.

They are free. Make one for your profile photo, one to share with family, and one to keep for yourself.

Before you start

A portrait with lantern light needs a good starting photo, so the setup is worth a minute of your time.

  • Use one clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight, face in focus and looking at the camera.
  • Keep the light soft and even in the starting photo, so the lantern glow blends in naturally.
  • Use a plain background in the starting photo. A busy room makes it harder to place you in a calm scene.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use photos of people who are happy for you to use them.

1. Holding a lantern at dusk

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show me holding a glowing lantern at dusk, with a soft purple sky behind me and warm light on my face. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of yourself.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under the photo.
  4. Send it and wait.
  5. Check your face first. If it looks wrong, try again with a clearer photo.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with the photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. If the light or the background looks wrong, ask for one small change and keep the rest the same.

Give it two or three tries. The best one is usually not the first.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use the clearest photo you have of yourself. A sharp starting photo keeps your face true.
  • Keep the light soft in the starting photo. Harsh flash fights with the lantern glow.
  • Start with the lantern at dusk or the window prompt. They are the most reliable.
  • Check the hands and the lantern in the result. Those are the spots where errors show up.
  • Ask for less in the scene if the first result looks busy. One lantern is enough.
  • Save the prompt that worked. You can use the same style for the next Ramadan.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo. The tool will guess that face, and you will notice.
  • Do not use a photo of another person without permission.
  • Do not change everything in the prompt at once. Change one thing and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same result every time. Portraits often need a second or third try.
  • Do not add food, parties or loud scenes to a Ramadan portrait. Keep it calm and respectful.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your photo, and use the portrait for your profile, a post, or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini often handles a single reference photo nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt well. Try the same prompt in both and keep the picture that looks most like you.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It usually comes from the starting photo. If it is dark, blurry or taken from the side, the tool guesses that face. Use a clear front photo of yourself, in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the outfit, the lantern or the setting to suit your photo. Keep the part about keeping your face the same, and keep the scene simple and calm.

Can I use the photo I make for a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a profile photo or a post on your own social media. Only use photos of people with their permission.
