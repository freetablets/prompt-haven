Start here: my pick

Start with Prompt 1, the dark sweater by the tree. It was the steadiest portrait in my tests, the dark sweater keeps the photo tidy, the tree lights add just enough warmth, and the face stays clear. If this one works, your starting photo is good.

Most Christmas portraits for men go wrong in the same way. Too much decoration, a silly hat, and a face you barely recognise. I have made that photo myself, and I would not send it to anyone.

So these prompts keep it simple. Start with a clear photo of yourself in daylight. The prompt adds a dark sweater, a coat in light snow, or a suit with a red tie, and keeps your face looking like you.

There is one Santa hat prompt here, and it is a soft smile, not a joke photo. The rest are calm, tidy portraits that work for a card, a profile photo or a print.

My first test used a photo taken in a car, at night, under a street light. The result looked like a stranger in a nice sweater. A daylight photo, same prompt, and suddenly it was me. That is the whole lesson of this page.

Before you start

A portrait lives or dies on the starting photo. Five points, learned the hard way.

  • Use a clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight, with your face in focus and looking at the camera.
  • Wear something plain in your starting photo. A plain shirt made it easy for the tool to add a sweater or a coat in my tests.
  • Keep the background simple, like a plain wall. A busy room makes the portrait harder.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use a photo you have the right to use.

I ran all ten with real photos, and the tidy ones won. Pick the portrait you like, and start with the tree if you are torn.

1. By the tree in a dark sweater

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you standing by a Christmas tree in a dark knit sweater, with warm lights and a calm smile. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under the photo.
  4. Send it and wait.
  5. Check the face first, then the outfit and the light. Save the portrait you like.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. If the outfit or the background is not quite right, ask for one small change only.

A portrait lives or dies on the face, so start with your best photo. A clear front photo in daylight will beat any amount of prompt editing, and I proved that to myself on this page.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use a photo where you are looking at the camera. A side view was the main reason a face changed in my tests.
  • Keep the outfit simple in the prompt. A dark sweater or a plain coat worked best in my runs.
  • Try the tree and fireplace prompts first. They were the most reliable.
  • Check the collar, the hands and the hair in the result. Those are the spots where I saw errors show up.
  • Ask for a vertical photo if you want it as a profile picture or a print.
  • Save the prompt that worked. It will give you a matching portrait next year.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not start with a dark or blurry photo. I did, and the tool guessed at the face, and the guess was wrong.
  • Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
  • Do not change the whole prompt at once. I changed the outfit and the background together once and learned nothing.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. Run it again if the first try is not right, my second run was usually better.
  • Do not add too many Christmas props. One tree or one fire was enough for a portrait in my tests.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the portrait for a card, a post, or a print. I tested every one before publishing it.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I run every prompt in both. Gemini is often good with a reference photo, and ChatGPT is often good with a clear, detailed prompt. Try the same prompt in both and keep the portrait you like best.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is usually the starting photo, and my car photo proved it. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess the face. Use a clear front photo in good light and keep the prompt as it is on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the outfit, the background or the light to fit your photo. Keep the part about keeping the face the same, and keep the scene simple. One change at a time is the safe way.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a card, a post on your own social media, or a print. Only use a photo you have the right to use.
