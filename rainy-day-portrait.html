Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 2 first, the window with a mug. Rain photos sound fun until you are the one getting wet. This one keeps you dry, the rain stays on the glass, and you still get that calm grey mood. It was the only one that worked on my first try.

Rain ruins most photo plans. Hair goes flat, light goes grey, and everyone ends up hiding under a shelter. But some of the best portraits I have seen were taken in the rain, because the light is soft and nobody is squinting.

These prompts lean into that. An umbrella on a quiet street, rain on a cafe window, a walk in light rain, reading by the window while it pours outside.

My first umbrella try was a disaster. I used a night photo, the tool could not find my face, and the umbrella came out looking like a tent. Daylight fixed it.

Start with a clear photo of yourself in decent light. The prompt adds the rain, the wet streets and the soft grey sky. Your face stays yours.

Before you start

Rain photos are all about soft light and simple scenes, so check these five things first. Grey light is kind, but it cannot rescue a bad photo.

  • Use a photo where your face is clear and lit from the front. Rain light is soft, and it cannot rescue a face lost in shadow.
  • Pick a starting photo taken in daylight. It gives the tool a clean face before it adds rain and grey skies.
  • Keep the background simple. A plain street or a quiet window suits rain better than a busy scene.
  • Copy the prompt exactly as it is the first time. Change one thing later if you need to, not five at once.
  • Save the best result, then try once more. Rain and light vary a little each run, and the second version is often the calmer one.

Choose the rainy scene you like most, copy the prompt, and paste it with your photo. The window one is my pick if you want to stay dry.

1. With an umbrella on a street

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you with an umbrella on a quiet street in light rain, in a warm coat, with soft light. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

These portraits are all about light, golden hour, spring sun, rain light or snow light. Bring a clear, well lit photo and let the prompt add the mood.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo taken in daylight. Your face should be in focus and lit from the front.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at the face first, then the light. If the face looks right but the light feels too strong or too weak, try the same prompt once more.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as softer light or a warmer tone, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

Light is the hardest thing to keep steady in rain photos, so try the same prompt in both tools if you have time. I kept the one where the light felt most natural, not the one with the most rain.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with the clearest photo you have. Daylight, face in focus, no shadow across your eyes.
  • Keep the prompt simple on the first try. A wordy prompt muddles soft light fast.
  • Change one thing at a time. Warmer light first, then the place, never both together.
  • Use a vertical photo for a profile photo or a print. Most prompts here ask for a 3 to 4 frame for that reason.
  • Check the eyes and the skin tone in the result. Strong light can wash a face out, and a second try often fixes it.
  • Save the prompt that worked. The same style will suit another rainy day photo later.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo as your starting photo. The tool will have to guess the face.
  • Do not upload a photo of another person without their permission. Only use photos you have the right to use.
  • Do not copy a prompt and then change every line at once. You will not know what went wrong.
  • Do not stop at one grey, muddy result. Run it again, the light often settles on the second go.
  • Do not ask for very strong light and a very busy scene at once. One strong idea per photo works better than two.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts on this page are free. Copy any of them, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the result for your own posts.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini often handles a reference photo nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt nicely. Try the same prompt in both and keep the calmer result.

Why does the face change in my photo?

The starting photo is usually the cause. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess at your face. Use a clear front photo in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the rain or the light to fit your photo, but keep the part about keeping your face the same. One change at a time is the safe way.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use. Post it on your own social media or use it as a profile photo. Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
