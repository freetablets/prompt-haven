New Year Portrait Prompts: 10 Ideas for 2026

Muhammad Afaq Verified Creator
Published: 2026-11-20 | Festive Prompts | Reading time: about 12 minutes

New Year is a strange, hopeful night. Old year behind you, new one ahead, and everyone dressed up for a fresh start. It deserves a better photo than a blurry one taken at midnight.

These prompts place you in the middle of that night. Fireworks, sparklers, confetti, a toast at midnight. Or the quiet morning after, when the year feels new and calm.

Start with a clear photo of yourself. A party photo works, but so does an ordinary one. The prompt adds the lights and the mood.

Pick the scene that fits your New Year. Loud and bright, or soft and hopeful. Either way, try one prompt first and see how it feels.

Before you start

New Year photos are full of light and movement, so these five points will help you get a clean result.

  • Use a photo where your face is clear and well lit. Party photos are often dark, so pick your brightest one.
  • Choose a starting photo with a simple background. The prompt adds fireworks and lights, and it needs room for them.
  • Keep your face facing the camera. A side view or a half hidden face will drift in the result.
  • Copy the prompt exactly as it is the first time. Change one thing later if you need to, not five at once.
  • Save the best result, then run it once more. Bright lights vary a little each run, and the second version is often the sharper one.

1. With fireworks behind you

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you with soft fireworks in the sky behind you, smiling, in a smart outfit, at night. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

These are once in a while photos, graduation and New Year. The moment matters, so start with your best photo from the day.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your clearest photo from the day. Your face should be in focus and facing the camera.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at the face first, then the outfit and the background. If the face looks right but the light is off, keep the photo and try the same prompt once more.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as warmer light or a simpler background, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

Both tools can give a different result from the same prompt. That is normal. Try the same prompt in both tools if you have time, and keep the photo you like best.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with the clearest photo you have. A photo taken in daylight, with the face in focus, gives the best result.
  • Keep the prompt simple on the first try. Long, complex prompts can confuse the tool and change the face.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you want a different outfit detail, change only that. If you want different light, change only the light.
  • Use a vertical photo for a profile photo or a print. Most of the prompts on this page ask for a vertical 3 to 4 frame for that reason.
  • Check hands, caps, hats and small details in the result. AI tools still make small errors there, and a second try often fixes them.
  • Save the prompt that worked for you. If you use the same prompt again with a new photo, the style will stay similar.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo as your starting photo. The tool will have to guess the face, and the result can look like a different person.
  • Do not upload a photo of another person without their permission. Only use photos you have the right to use.
  • Do not copy a prompt and then change every line at once. You will not know what went wrong if the result is poor.
  • Do not expect the same result every time. AI tools vary, even with the same prompt and the same photo.
  • Do not pile on props and lights and outfits all at once. One clear idea per photo works better than three.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts on this page are free. Copy any of them, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the result for your own cards and posts.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini often handles a reference photo nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt nicely. Try the same prompt in both and keep the brighter result.

Why does the face change in my photo?

The starting photo is usually the cause. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess at your face. Use a clear front photo in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the outfit, the lights or the setting to match your own New Year, but keep the part about keeping your face the same. One change at a time is the safe way.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use. Share it with friends and family or post it on your own social media. Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
