Graduation Portrait Prompts: 10 Ideas for 2026

Muhammad Afaq Verified Creator
Published: 2026-11-14 | Festive Prompts | Reading time: about 12 minutes
Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 4 first, the one where you hold your diploma. My first cap toss photo gave me three caps, two hands I did not recognise, and a smile that was not mine. The diploma shot is calmer. Your hands have something real to hold, the gown sits still, and your face stays yours.

My graduation photos were a mess. The cap landed half out of frame, my mum was waving from three rows back, and the one photo where I was actually smiling had a stranger's elbow in it.

So I stopped trying to fix those photos. I took the clearest one I had, a plain phone shot from before the ceremony, and let the prompt rebuild the moment around it. Gown, cap, diploma, the lot.

That is what these ten prompts do. They keep your face the same and put you back in the bits that mattered. The walk across the stage. Your family beside you. The cap going up.

Got a photo from the day that is almost good? Start there. And if your gown is wrinkled or your tassel is on the wrong side, do not worry. The prompt sorts the outfit. You just need a face it can see clearly.

Before you start

A graduation photo ends up on walls and in frames, so give it one minute of prep. These five points are the ones that saved my own tries.

  • Use the clearest photo from the day, face in focus, looking at the camera. A photo taken in shade beats a sunny one where you are squinting.
  • If your gown and cap are in the photo, keep them in frame. The tool copies the outfit better when it can see it.
  • For the family and friends shots, use one clear photo per person. A cousin cut off at the edge will come back as a stranger.
  • Copy the prompt word for word on the first go. I changed three things at once on my first try and had no idea which change broke the cap.
  • Run the same prompt twice and keep the better one. My second run fixed the tassel, the smile and the gown all at once.

10 copy and paste prompts

Pick the moment you want back, copy the prompt, and paste it with your photo. I would start with the diploma shot, but the cap toss is the fun one.

1. In a gown and cap, smiling

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you in a graduation gown and cap, smiling, with a plain bright background. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

These are once in a while photos, graduation and New Year. The moment matters, so start with your best photo from the day.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your clearest photo from the day. Your face should be in focus and facing the camera.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at the face first, then the outfit and the background. If the face looks right but the light is off, keep the photo and try the same prompt once more.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as warmer light or a simpler background, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

I ran the diploma prompt in both tools on the same photo. Gemini kept my face closer. ChatGPT got the gown neater. Neither was wrong, so keep the one that looks most like you on the day.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with a daylight photo where your face is sharp. Morning light before the ceremony is kinder than harsh midday sun.
  • Check the tassel in the result. It should hang on one side, not float across your forehead.
  • Change one thing at a time. Want a different background? Change only that, then look again.
  • Use a vertical frame for a print or a profile photo. That is why these prompts ask for 3 to 4.
  • Look at the hands and the cap brim. Those are the two places where odd shapes creep in, and a second run often fixes them.
  • Save the prompt that worked. Next year, when your sister graduates, the same style will match.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not start with a dark hall photo where your face is a shadow. The tool will invent a face, and it will not be yours.
  • Do not upload a photo of someone else without asking them first. Only use photos you have the right to use.
  • Do not rewrite the whole prompt after one bad result. Change one line and try again.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. Even the same prompt and photo can come out different.
  • Do not pile on confetti, balloons, banners and a sunset all at once. One clear idea per photo works better.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts on this page are free. Copy any of them, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the result for your own cards, posts and prints.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I test with both. Gemini often keeps a reference face closer, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt closely. Try the same prompt in both and keep the photo that looks most like you on the day.

Why does the face change in my photo?

The starting photo is usually the cause. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess at your face. Use a clear front photo in good light, with your face fully in view and your cap sitting straight.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the background, the gown details or the light to match your day, but keep the part about keeping your face the same. One change at a time is the safe way, so you can see what helped.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use. Share it with family, post it on your own social media, or print and frame it. Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
