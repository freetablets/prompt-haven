Start here: my pick

Start with Prompt 1, sleeping on a soft blanket. I tested it with a photo of my cousin's baby boy, asleep in the morning light, and it came back so calm I left it alone. Sleep is the easiest scene for a one month old, because he does not have to do anything at all.

The first month goes by so fast that most of the photos I see from new parents are blurry ones taken at 3 in the morning. These prompts are for the one clear photo you did manage to get, the one where he is calm and the light is kind.

I tested this set with photos my cousin sent me of his son. The ones that worked were the plain ones, a white blanket, a quiet room, his face clear. The one that failed was taken under a bright ceiling light, and his skin came out looking flat and washed out.

I kept these prompts gentle on purpose. No loud colours, no odd props, nothing that would make a newborn look uncomfortable. Just soft light, a plain background, and your baby as the centre of it.

Use them for a keepsake, a print for the grandparents, or the first photo you share with family. They are free, and you can try as many as you like. And if he wakes up and yawns halfway through, keep that photo too.

Before you start

Newborn photos need a gentle hand, so sort these five things before you start.

  • Use one clear photo of him in soft, gentle light, face in focus. Harsh ceiling light washes a newborn's skin out.
  • Keep the background plain in the starting photo. A plain blanket or a plain wall works best.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one small thing at a time.
  • Only use a photo of your own baby, or one a parent has said you can use.
  • Pick a photo where he is calm, asleep or just awake. A crying photo is hard for the tool to turn into a calm one.

Pick the scene that fits the photo you already have, asleep, awake, or yawning, and paste the prompt under it.

1. Sleeping on a soft blanket

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show your baby boy sleeping on a soft white blanket, with calm light and a plain background. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo of your baby, taken in soft daylight.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under the photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. Look at the face and the skin first. If they look right, save the picture before you try another version.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your baby's photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with the photo.
  4. Send it and wait.
  5. If the outfit or the background is not quite right, ask for one small change and leave the rest alone.

If the first result looks too smooth or too perfect, swap in a softer starting photo, one taken in morning light near a window. That one change brought my test photos back to looking like a real baby, not a doll.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use a photo taken in soft morning light near a window. It was the single biggest improvement in my tests.
  • Keep his outfit and the blanket pale and plain. Loud colours pull the eye away from his face.
  • Start with the sleeping prompt. It is the most reliable, because he is still and the scene is simple.
  • Check his fingers, his ears and the edge of the blanket in the result. Those are the spots where small errors show up.
  • Ask for a vertical photo if it is going in a frame or on a card. A wide crop loses the softness.
  • Save the prompt that worked. The same style will suit his two month photo too, and the pair will look good together.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a photo taken under a harsh ceiling light. His skin comes out washed out, and the tool cannot put the softness back.
  • Do not use a photo of a baby without a parent saying it is fine. That rule is not negotiable.
  • Do not add too many props at once. A basket, a blanket, a bear and a sign in one photo is too much. Pick one.
  • Do not rewrite the whole prompt at once. Change one thing, the blanket or the toy, and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. I ran the sleeping prompt three times and got three slightly different babies. Keep the one that looks most like him.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free, plus the 3 bonus ones further down the page. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with his photo, and use the picture for a keepsake, a post, or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I use both. Gemini kept the baby's face closer on the sleeping prompt, while ChatGPT followed the basket scene more closely. Run the same prompt in both and keep the picture that looks most like him.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is almost always the starting photo. If it is dark, blurry, or taken from the side, the tool has to guess the face. Use a clear front photo in soft light and leave the prompt alone on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the outfit, the blanket, or the toy to fit your photo. Keep the line about keeping the face the same, and keep the scene simple so the tool has less to get wrong.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a keepsake, a post on your own social media, or a print for the grandparents. Only use a photo of a baby with a parent saying it is fine.
