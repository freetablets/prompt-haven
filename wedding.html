Wedding Photo Prompts: 10 Ideas for Gemini and ChatGPT 2026

Muhammad Afaq Verified Creator
Published: 2026-10-06 | Couple Prompts | Reading time: about 12 minutes

A wedding day is over before you know it. You spend months planning it, and then it passes in a rush of hugs, photos and happy tears. The pictures are what is left when the day is done.

These prompts are for those pictures. They keep your faces the same and place you in the moments that matter, in the garden, holding hands at sunset, the first dance, the cake, the doorway of the church.

Start with your clearest photo from the day. A phone photo works fine if faces are clear and the light is decent. The prompt adds the setting and the warmth.

Pick the moment that means the most to you first. For most couples it is the sunset one, or the first dance. Start there.

Before you start

Wedding photos are keepers, so take a minute on these five points before you copy a prompt.

  • Start with your clearest photo from the day. Both faces should be in focus, in good light, and facing the camera.
  • Upload one clear photo per person when the tool asks. A face that is cut off or turned away will come back wrong.
  • Keep the starting background simple. A plain wall or an open garden gives the tool a clean base for a wedding scene.
  • Copy the prompt exactly as it is the first time. Change one thing later if you need to, not five at once.
  • Save the best result, then run the same prompt once more. Wedding light varies a little each run, and the second version is often the one you frame.

Choose the moment that means the most to you, copy the prompt, and paste it with your photos.

1. In wedding outfits in a garden

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you as a couple in simple wedding outfits in a garden, with flowers and soft daylight. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

Couple photos need two good starting photos, one per person. That is the one step people skip, and it is the one that matters most.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of each person. Both faces should be in focus and facing the camera.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photos.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at both faces first, then the hands and the background. If one face looks right and the other does not, try the same prompt once more with clearer photos.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of each person in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photos.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as warmer light or a simpler background, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

Both tools can give a different result from the same prompt. That is normal with couple photos, because two faces have to stay true at once. Try the same prompt in both tools if you have time, and keep the photo you like best.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with the clearest photos you have. Daylight photos, with both faces in focus, give the best result.
  • Keep the prompt simple on the first try. Long, complex prompts can confuse the tool and change a face.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you want different light, change only the light. If you want a different setting, change only the setting.
  • Use a vertical photo for a profile photo or a print. Most of the prompts on this page ask for a vertical 3 to 4 frame for that reason.
  • Check hands, rings and small details in the result. AI tools still make small errors there, and a second try often fixes them.
  • Save the prompt that worked for you. If you use the same prompt again with new photos, the style will stay similar.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo as your starting photo. The tool will have to guess a face, and the result can look like a different person.
  • Do not upload a photo of another person without their permission. Only use photos you have the right to use.
  • Do not copy a prompt and then change every line at once. You will not know what went wrong if the result is poor.
  • Do not expect the same result every time. AI tools vary, even with the same prompt and the same photos.
  • Do not use only one photo for a couple scene and expect both faces to stay true. Two clear photos give a far better result.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts on this page are free. Copy any of them, paste them into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photos, and use the result for your own cards, prints and posts.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini often handles reference photos nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt nicely. Try the same prompt in both and keep the warmer result.

Why does the face change in my photo?

The starting photos are usually the cause. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess at a face. Use clear front photos of both people, in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the outfits or the light to match your own day, but keep the part about keeping faces the same. One change at a time is the safe way.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use. Print them, frame them, share them with family or post them on your own social media. Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
