Start here: my pick

Start with Prompt 2, holding hands at sunset. I tested it with a photo my cousin sent me from her wedding, taken on a phone in the garden, and the sunset light did something lovely to it. Their faces stayed theirs, and the photo looked like the moment, not a pose.

A wedding day is over before you know it. You spend months planning it, and then it passes in a rush of hugs, photos and happy tears. The pictures are what is left when the day is done, so I wanted these prompts to protect the ones that matter.

My cousin's first try went wrong in a way I did not expect. She used a group photo where the light was mixed, sun on one side and shade on the other, and the tool gave her husband a face that was almost his, but not quite. We switched to a clear photo of just the two of them, faces in focus, and everything settled down.

These prompts keep your faces the same and place you in the moments that matter, in the garden, holding hands at sunset, the first dance, the cake, the doorway of the church. Start with your clearest photo from the day. A phone photo works fine if faces are clear and the light is decent.

Pick the moment that means the most to you first. For most couples it is the sunset one, or the first dance. Start there, and let the rest follow.

Before you start

Wedding photos carry a lot of feeling, so a minute of setup here is worth it.

  • Use a clear photo of both of you, faces in focus, in good light. Mixed sun and shade is what broke my cousin's first try.
  • Keep the background plain in your starting photo if you can. A tidy garden or a plain wall beats a crowded hall.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use photos of people who are happy for you to use them.
  • Pick the photo where you both look relaxed, not the one where you are both posing hard.

Find the moment that means the most to you, copy the prompt, and paste it with your wedding photo.

1. In wedding outfits in a garden

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you as a couple in simple wedding outfits in a garden, with flowers and soft daylight. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

Couple photos need two good starting photos, one per person. That is the one step people skip, and it is the one that matters most.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of each person. Both faces should be in focus and facing the camera.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photos.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at both faces first, then the hands and the background. If one face looks right and the other does not, try the same prompt once more with clearer photos.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of each person in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photos.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as warmer light or a simpler background, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

If a result comes back looking stiff, do not blame the photo. Try the sunset or the first dance prompt with the same starting photo. A moment with movement in it loosened up my cousin's smile more than any wording change did.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use the clearest photo you have of both of you, faces in focus. One weak face can spoil the whole picture.
  • Keep the light similar in your starting photos. Mixed sun and shade is what broke my cousin's first try.
  • Start with the sunset or the garden prompts. They are the most reliable, because the light is warm and steady.
  • Check both faces, the hands, and the edge of the dress in the result. Those are the spots where small errors show up.
  • Ask for a vertical photo if it is going in a frame or on a card. A wide crop loses the feeling.
  • Save the prompt that worked. You can run the same style on another photo from the day, and the pair will sit nicely together.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a photo where one face is in sun and the other is in shade. The tool struggles, and one face will drift.
  • Do not use a photo of someone without their permission. Wedding photos belong to the people in them.
  • Do not rewrite the whole prompt at once. Change one thing, the setting or the light, and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. I ran the sunset prompt three times and got three different evenings. Keep the best.
  • Do not add too many details at once. A garden, a lake, lights and a cake in one photo is too much. Pick one scene.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free, plus the 3 bonus ones further down the page. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your wedding photos, and use the picture for a keepsake, a post, or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I use both. Gemini kept both faces closer on the sunset prompt, while ChatGPT followed the garden scene more closely. Run the same prompt in both and keep the picture that looks most like the two of you.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is almost always the starting photos. If one is dark, blurry, or taken from the side, the tool guesses that face. Use clear front photos of both people in good light and leave the prompt alone on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the outfits, or the light to fit your photos. Keep the line about keeping both faces the same, and keep the scene simple so the tool has less to get wrong.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a keepsake, a post on your own social media, or a print. Only use photos of people who are happy for you to use them.
