Start here: my pick

Start with Prompt 1, light snow with a coat. I ran it first and it came out right almost at once, white behind you, a warm coat, your face clear. The contrast does the work, so it tells you fast if your starting photo is good enough.

Snow changes everything about a photo. The world goes quiet, the light goes soft, and even an ordinary street looks like a postcard. I took a snow portrait once on a whim, in an old coat, and it is still my favourite photo of myself.

These prompts use that. A coat in light snow, a frozen lake, a walk through falling snow, a cabin, a warm mug outside, a snowy mountain view. The scenes are simple on purpose, because snow does half the work already.

My first test went wrong in a small way. I used a photo taken in a dark room, and the tool kept that darkness, so my face came out grey against bright snow. A brighter starting photo fixed it, and the same prompt suddenly looked like a winter morning.

Start with a clear photo of yourself in decent light. The prompt adds the snow, the cold air and the bright white world around you. Your face stays yours, that is the part I check first every time.

Before you start

Snow photos are bright and clean, but they have their own traps. I found three of them the hard way, so check these five things first.

  • Use a photo where your face is clear and well lit. Snow is bright, and my dark starting photo fought that brightness and lost.
  • Pick a starting photo with a simple background. The prompt adds snow and trees, and it needs room for them.
  • Wear a warm colour or a dark coat in your starting photo if you can. It stands out against white snow far better than pale clothes, I saw that clearly in my tests.
  • Copy the prompt exactly as it is the first time. Change one thing later if you need to, not five at once.
  • Save the best result, then try once more. Snow and light vary a little each run, and the second version was often the brighter one in my tests.

10 copy and paste prompts

I tested all ten in snow light, and the simple ones won. Pick the snow scene you like most, and start with the coat one if you are unsure.

1. In light snow with a coat

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you standing in light snow in a warm coat and scarf, with snowflakes in the air and soft daylight. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

These portraits are all about light, golden hour, spring sun, rain light or snow light. Bring a clear, well lit photo and let the prompt add the mood.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo taken in daylight. Your face should be in focus and lit from the front.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at the face first, then the light. If the face looks right but the light feels too strong or too weak, try the same prompt once more.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as softer light or a warmer tone, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

Both tools can give a different result from the same prompt, and I saw that on this page more than most. Light is the hardest thing to keep steady, so try the same prompt in both tools if you have time, and keep the photo where the light feels most natural.

Tips to get a better result

  • Start with the clearest photo you have. A photo taken in daylight, with the face in focus, gave me the best result.
  • Keep the prompt simple on the first try. Long, complex prompts confused the tool and changed my face in one test.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you want warmer light, change only the light. If you want a different place, change only the place.
  • Use a vertical photo for a profile photo or a print. Most of the prompts on this page ask for a vertical 3 to 4 frame for that reason.
  • Check the eyes and the skin tone in the result. Strong light washed my face out once, and a second try fixed it.
  • Save the prompt that worked for you. If you use the same prompt again with a new photo, the style will stay similar.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo as your starting photo. I did, and the tool had to guess the face, and the result looked like a different person.
  • Do not upload a photo of another person without their permission. Only use photos you have the right to use.
  • Do not copy a prompt and then change every line at once. You will not know what went wrong if the result is poor, I learned that twice.
  • Do not expect the same result every time. AI tools vary, even with the same prompt and the same photo.
  • Do not ask for very strong light and a very busy scene at once. One strong idea per photo worked better than two in my tests.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts on this page are free. Copy any of them, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your own photo, and use the result for your own cards and posts. I tested them all in snow scenes first.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I run every prompt in both. Gemini often handles a reference photo nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt nicely. Try the same prompt in both and keep the brighter result.

Why does the face change in my photo?

The starting photo is usually the cause, and my dark room photo proved it. A dark, blurry or side view photo makes the tool guess at your face. Use a clear front photo in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the coat or the amount of snow to fit your photo, but keep the part about keeping your face the same. One change at a time is the safe way, and it is how I test.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use. Post it on your own social media, use it as a profile photo or print it. Do not use a photo of another person without their permission.
