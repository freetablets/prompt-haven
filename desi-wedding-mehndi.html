Start here: my pick

Try Prompt 1 first, showing your henna hands with marigolds behind you. It is the classic mehndi photo, and it works on the first try more often than the dancing scenes.

A mehndi is the loudest, happiest part of a Desi wedding. The marigolds are everywhere, the dhol is playing, and someone is always dancing, even the aunties who said they would only watch. A photo should feel like that.

These prompts start from your own photo. The tool puts you in a yellow or green outfit, henna on your hands, marigolds behind you, and keeps your face exactly the same. That matters, because a mehndi photo is one you will look at for years.

I tested these with a photo of my cousin first. The dancing one took three tries, because my starting photo was taken from the side and the henna kept landing on the wrong hand. A clear front photo fixed it. Start with a good one and save yourself the trouble.

They are free. Make one for your mehndi invitation, one for the family group, and one to print for your mum.

Before you start

Henna and bright colours need a good starting photo, so the setup is worth a minute of your time.

  • Use one clear photo of yourself, taken in daylight, face in focus and looking at the camera.
  • Keep the light soft and even in the starting photo, so the yellow and green colours come out true.
  • Use a plain background in the starting photo. A busy room makes it harder to place you in a mehndi scene.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use photos of people who are happy for you to use them.

How to use these prompts

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload one clear photo of yourself.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under the photo.
  4. Send it and wait.
  5. Check your face first. If it looks wrong, try again with a clearer photo.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with the photo.
  4. Send it and wait for the result.
  5. If the light or the background looks wrong, ask for one small change and keep the rest the same.

Give it two or three tries. The best one is usually not the first.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use the clearest photo you have of yourself. A sharp starting photo keeps your face true.
  • Keep the light soft in the starting photo, so the yellow and green stay true.
  • Start with the henna hands or marigold plate prompts. They are the most reliable.
  • Check the hands closely in the result. Henna is the hardest detail to get right.
  • Ask for fewer flowers if the first result looks busy. Marigolds should frame you, not hide you.
  • Save the prompt that worked. You can use the same style for the next wedding in the family.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use a dark, blurry or side view photo. The tool will guess that face, and you will notice.
  • Do not use a photo of another person without permission.
  • Do not change everything in the prompt at once. Change one thing and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same result every time. Henna details often need a second or third try.
  • Do not add too many flowers or colours to the scene. Marigolds and one bright outfit are enough.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your photo, and use the picture for an invitation, a post, or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well. Gemini often handles a single reference photo nicely, and ChatGPT often follows a detailed prompt well. Try the same prompt in both and keep the picture that looks most like you.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It usually comes from the starting photo. If it is dark, blurry or taken from the side, the tool guesses that face. Use a clear front photo of yourself, in good light.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the outfit colour, the flowers or the setting to suit your photo. Keep the part about keeping your face the same, and keep the scene simple.

Can I use the photo I make for an invitation or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a mehndi invitation, a gift print, or a post on your own social media. Only use photos of people with their permission.
