Golden Hour Portrait Prompts: 10 Warm Ideas for 2026

Start here: my pick

Start with Prompt 1, in a field at sunset. I tested it with a plain photo of myself taken in a car park, of all places, and the warm light turned it into something I actually liked. The field, the tall grass, the low sun, it all sat around my face without changing it.

There is a short window before sunset when the light turns warm and everything looks better. Photographers call it golden hour. The rest of us just know it as the time when phone photos suddenly look good, and I have chased that window more times than I can count.

The first time I tested this set, I used a photo taken at noon, harsh light, hard shadows, and the result looked like someone had painted gold over a midday photo. It did not work. I swapped in a photo taken in the late afternoon, same jacket, same face, and the warm light finally made sense.

These prompts borrow that light. You start with an ordinary photo of yourself, and the prompt places you in that warm evening glow, in a field, by the sea, on a rooftop, or in a garden. You do not need to have been outside at sunset to use them.

Try one scene first. Golden hour suits almost everyone, but the setting matters, so pick the place that feels most like you and start there. A field, a rooftop, a balcony, whichever one you would actually stand in.

Before you start

Golden hour is all about the light, so get these five things right before you start.

  • Use a clear photo of you in soft light, face in focus, looking at the camera. A noon photo with hard shadows fights the warm light.
  • Wear something simple in your starting photo. A plain top lets the warm light do the work.
  • Keep the background plain in your starting photo. A busy street behind you fights with the sunset.
  • Copy the prompt exactly on the first try, then change one thing at a time.
  • Only use a photo of yourself, or of someone who is happy for you to use it.

Pick the golden hour place that feels most like you, copy the prompt, and paste it under your photo.

1. In a field at sunset

Create a realistic, natural photo from my uploaded photo. Keep the face exactly the same as in the uploaded photo, with the same eyes, smile, hair and skin tone. Do not change the person into someone else, and do not make the skin look smooth or plastic. Show you in a field at sunset, with tall grass, warm gold light on your face, and a soft sky. Keep the pose relaxed and natural, with a real expression, not a stiff studio pose. Use soft, natural light that suits the scene, with clear detail in the face, hair and clothes. Keep the background simple so the person stays the main focus. Shoot it in a vertical 3 to 4 frame, as if taken on a 35mm lens with a shallow depth of field. The final photo should look like a real photo taken on a good day, with true colours, gentle shadows and a calm, honest feel. It should work well for a profile photo, a print, or a post with family and friends.

How to use these prompts

These portraits are all about light, golden hour, spring sun, rain light or snow light. Bring a clear, well lit photo and let the prompt add the mood.

How to use a prompt in Google Gemini

  1. Open Gemini on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload a clear photo taken in daylight. Your face should be in focus and lit from the front.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it under your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result. Do not send the same prompt again while it is still working.
  5. Look at the face first, then the light. If the face looks right but the light feels too strong or too weak, try the same prompt once more.

How to use a prompt in ChatGPT

  1. Open ChatGPT on your phone or computer.
  2. Upload your photo in the chat.
  3. Copy one prompt from this page and paste it with your photo.
  4. Send it, and wait for the result.
  5. If the result is close but not quite right, ask for one small change, such as softer light or a warmer tone, and keep the rest of the prompt the same.

If the warm light in your result looks painted on, swap in a softer starting photo, one taken in the late afternoon. That one change fixed the fake gold look in my tests more than any prompt tweak did.

Tips to get a better result

  • Use a photo taken in soft late afternoon light if you can. A noon photo with hard shadows fights the warm light.
  • Keep your outfit simple in the starting photo. A plain top lets the sunset do the work.
  • Start with the field or the garden prompts. They are the most reliable, because the scene is simple and the light is steady.
  • Check your hair and the edge of your face in the result. That is where the warm light sometimes goes wrong.
  • Ask for a vertical photo if it is going on a post or a phone screen. A wide crop loses the glow.
  • Save the prompt that worked. The same golden hour style will suit a different place next time.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not start with a photo taken at noon. The hard shadows fight the warm light, and the result looks painted on.
  • Do not add too much sunset at once. A field, a sea, a rooftop and a lake in one photo is too much. Pick one place.
  • Do not rewrite the whole prompt at once. Change one thing, the setting or the light, and see what happens.
  • Do not expect the same picture twice. I ran the field prompt three times and got three different sunsets. Keep the best.
  • Do not use a busy street as your starting background. The tool has to remove it, and the sunset ends up in odd places.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these prompts for free?

Yes, all 10 prompts are free, plus the 3 bonus ones further down the page. Copy one, paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT with your photo, and use the picture for a post or a print.

Which tool works best with these prompts?

Both work well, and I use both. Gemini kept my face closer on the field prompt, while ChatGPT followed the sea light better. Run the same prompt in both and keep the picture you like best.

Why does the face change in my photo?

It is almost always the starting photo. If it is dark, blurry, or taken from the side, the tool has to guess the face. Use a clear front photo in good light and leave the prompt alone on the first try.

Can I change the prompt for my own photo?

Yes. Change the setting, the outfit, or the light to fit your photo. Keep the line about keeping the face the same, and keep the scene simple so the tool has less to get wrong.

Can I use the photo I make for a card or a post?

Yes, for your own personal use, like a post on your own social media or a print. Only use a photo of someone else if they are happy for you to use it.
